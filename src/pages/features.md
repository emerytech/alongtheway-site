> **A first look.** Along the Way is in a free pilot through TestFlight. These features describe the app in development, and a test build may differ. [Join the pilot →](/pilot/)

## Learning time

Record the subject, length, day, and children taking part in a learning activity. Mark whether it happened at home, add a lesson description or notes, and attach photos of the work. An activity can count for more than one child.

See time by subject and progress for each child’s school year. Open a logged entry to make a correction, or set your own targets.

### Weekly goals

Set how much time a week you’d like for a subject, and see the week fill in day by day on each child’s page.

### An hours record to print or keep

Make a PDF for a child’s school year: total hours against your targets, hours by subject, school days by quarter, and, if you want it, the daily log. It is made on your phone and handed to you to print, save, or send. The app doesn’t send it anywhere.

### Core and Non-Core subjects

Keep subjects grouped as Core and Non-Core. Add your own subjects, rename them, change their classification, or hide ones you aren’t using. Hiding a subject keeps its recorded learning time.

### A starting point for state planning

Built-in guidance currently covers **Missouri and Iowa**, with options for different instruction arrangements. It suggests the applicable hours, days, or subject targets; parents can change or clear their child’s targets. Families in other states can still record learning time and set their own goals.

**State information is a planning aid, not legal advice.** Requirements depend on your circumstances and can change. Check current official sources and obtain qualified advice when needed. The app does not certify compliance.

## Day planner

Lay out a day for the whole family or a particular child. Add a subject, lesson, date, and optional length. You can copy an item to additional days in the week.

Mark an item **Done** to record it as learning time. If you haven’t entered a length, the app asks for it then. You don’t have to enter the same lesson twice.

## Daily log

Below the day’s plan, see **“What was done this day”**: logged activities grouped by child, with each child’s total time. The log includes activities you planned and learning you recorded as the day unfolded.

## Keepsakes

Save photographs of your child’s artwork and projects in a private family archive. Choose images from your photo library, then add or edit a title, date, and note.

Keepsakes stay with your family. They are separate from village photos and cannot be shared to the village from inside the app. Location and EXIF metadata are removed when images are processed for storage.

## Bible reading

The optional built-in reader includes the **Berean Standard Bible (BSB)** and **King James Version (KJV)**. The Bible text is bundled in the app, so the text itself does not need to be downloaded while you read.

Choose the family or a child as the reader, open a book and chapter, and mark chapters as read. See chapter progress and continue from where you left off. Saving or changing reading progress requires a connection to the app’s server.

Switch Bible reading on in Settings when it fits your family. It starts switched off. Reading progress is private to your family, and reading a chapter does not automatically log learning hours.

## Book log

Keep a list of the books each child is reading and has finished, with the author, the date finished, and a short report in their own words.

## A child’s own checklist

A parent can let a child see their own day: the plan to tick off, their books, and the Bible reader if your family uses it. Nothing else. Switch it on for one child with a code for their own device, or set up a shared **kids’ tablet** once, where each child taps their own name. An optional PIN keeps siblings out of each other’s profiles; it is a privacy gate between children, not a security lock.

A child never sees the village, another family, settings, or billing.

## Your village

Connect with another homeschool family using a village code. Your village shows connected families and their parents’ display names. Either family can remove a connection.

Parents can choose photos to share with their connected families. These photos are separate from academic records: they have no captions, child-name fields, tags, or links to a child’s record. Connected families may save a copy before a photo expires. See the [draft privacy policy](/privacy/#photos) for visibility and retention details.

### Events and meet-ups

Plan a field trip or get-together, share it with the families you choose, and see who is coming by headcount. The host can set a pin for the meeting place. On the day, a parent may choose to share where their own phone is with the invited families while the event is open. It is off every time until switched on, and the app never tracks location in the background.

Your children’s learning records, keepsakes, and Bible reading progress stay inside your family.

## Getting started

A new family is walked through a few short steps: home state, children, school year, village, and Bible reading. Each can be skipped. A short list on the Children tab then points to what to try next, and ticks itself off as you go.

## Family join

A **family join code** brings a second parent into the same family, with access to that family’s records. This is different from a village code, which connects two separate families.

If you already have an account, use **Family join** in Settings. Joining another family moves your account; it does not combine records from two families. The app asks for confirmation if you are the last parent leaving a family whose children and records would be deleted.

## Optional detail

For families who want it, courses, assignments, grades, grade history, and attendance records are built into the app. Grade tracking starts switched off and can be enabled in Settings. Switching it off hides the grade section without deleting its records.
